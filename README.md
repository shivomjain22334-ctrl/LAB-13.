### LAB 13 – RAG and Source Verification

In Lab 13, I learned about **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** and the importance of grounding AI answers in official documents. The activity compared an ungrounded AI answer with an answer generated using an uploaded SkyLine Airways Passenger Service Charter.

The ungrounded answer incorrectly invented a 50% cash refund, a ₹2,500 travel voucher, and hotel accommodation. After the official policy document was provided, the grounded answer correctly identified that domestic delays exceeding two hours qualified for complimentary refreshments and hot meals, while refunds or rebooking applied only when delays exceeded four hours.

The lab also introduced a five-step verification routine: identify the claim, find the supporting source, verify the match, inspect the context, and determine whether the information can be used. This activity taught me that RAG and source verification can reduce hallucination risks and make aviation communication more factually reliable and defensible.
